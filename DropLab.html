    Models & references

    DropLab provides analytical and numerical screening for early chip design. Presets are calculated examples, not proven fabrication recipes. Diagrams are original parameter-linked schematics, not fabrication masks.

    Droplet generator

    T-junction: L = wc (1 + α Qd/Qc), V ≈ L wc h, deq = (6V/π)1/3, f = Qd/V. The equivalent sphere converts volume; it is not the confined drop width. Application screening limits are now Ca < 0.1 and both inlet Re < 10. Size is suppressed beyond those limits, at h > wc, or outside 1 ≤ wc/wd ≤ 4. Results above the original conservative Ca 0.01 / Re 1 screens are explicitly marked as extrapolation. Changing a screening limit does not validate the squeezing model at higher Ca or Re. α and the plug-volume approximation need calibration.

    Flow focusing: d = dref [(Qd/Qc)/qref]b, V = πd³/6. Default coefficients are illustrative, not measured. Geometry and fluid-property effects on size are not predicted. Qc is the total of two symmetric continuous inlets; wc is their summed width and lc one branch length. A sphere is shown separately at the common physical scale; the downstream confined shape is not modeled.

    Inverse diameter design: enter equivalent-volume sphere diameter and one phase flow, then calculate the other. T-junction ratio q = [π d_eq³/(6 wc² h) − 1]/α; positive flow requires d_eq > (6 wc² h/π)1/3. Flow-focusing ratio q = qref (d_eq/dref)1/b. With Qc known, Qd = q Qc; with Qd known, Qc = Qd/q. The same screening, calibration and extrapolation rules apply to the solved operating point. A target match is algebraic, not an experimental accuracy claim.

    Pressure input: enter continuous and dispersed inlet gauge pressures Pc and Pd. Optional junction backpressure Pj defaults to 0 kPa gauge; both inlets must exceed it. Qd = (Pd − Pj)/Rd. For T-junctions, Qc = (Pc − Pj)/Rc. For symmetric flow focusing, each continuous side has Rside and Qc = 2(Pc − Pj)/Rside. Rc, Rd and Rside use the rigid rectangular-duct odd Fourier series. Pressure is converted from kPa to Pa internally; volume flow is converted to µL/min.

    This is a fully developed, single-phase inlet resistance estimate, not a solved two-phase downstream pressure network. Junction losses, capillary pressure jumps/entry, external tubing, downstream droplet resistance and wall deformation are excluded. If downstream geometry or measured Pj is unknown, inlet gauge pressures alone do not uniquely specify the actual chip flow; Pj = 0 is an explicit simplifying assumption.

    Concentration gradient generator

    Resistive mixing tree: Kirchhoff flow balance solves a split-and-merge lattice of rectangular resistors. Segment length = base + pitch count × added length. P1/P2/P3 repeat down the rows; two imposed inlet flows drain to ambient outlets. Solute follows solved pressure directions through perfectly mixed nodes. Residence = active network volume / total flow. The first-mode diffusion score M = 1 − exp(−π²Dt/w²) is a screening proxy, not a measured mixing index or a local mixing guarantee. It does not modify outlet means.

    Six-inlet co-flow: U ∂C/∂x = D ∂²C/∂y², U = Q/(Wh), no-flux side walls, flow-weighted inlet bands, no axial diffusion. A conservative implicit finite-volume solver uses 120 transverse cells × 80 axial steps and exact initial cell-average overlap. Mixing score = 1 − σout/σin. Sampling bins are mathematical regions in one chamber, not physical outlet branches. Six parallel inlet bands independently simplify the paper's detailed inlet manifold. Inlet pressure losses are excluded; inlet velocities use the entered inlet width and depth.

    Programmable dilution bank: stock fraction f = (Ctarget − CB)/(CA − CB), q = Qtotal/N. Two ideal equal-pressure supply buses feed each mixer. Rfeed = (ΔP − Rmix q)/(fraction × q); feed length follows rectangular resistance per length. Pure endpoints close one feed. Target agreement assumes complete mixing, identical fluids, ambient outlets, and negligible supply-bus, bend and junction losses. RMSE is a forward algebraic check, not experimental accuracy.

    Capillary diagnostics: Pc = 2γ cosθ (1/w + 1/h) with identical contact angle on all four walls. Isolated-channel fill time = r′ wh L²/(2Pc), only for positive Pc. The diagnostic excludes network transients, finite reservoirs, contact-angle hysteresis and synchronized fronts; the tree is still driven by imposed flow.

    The supplied papers

    1. Khalghollah et al. (2026). Small 22, e73821. Machine Learning-Driven Capillary Microfluidic Design Automation for Programmable Gradient Generation and Antimicrobial Testing. Informs the tree concept, width/length/pitch controls, capillary screening and six-level target example. Its transient multiphase CFD, synchronized filling, neural network and experiments are not reproduced.
    2. Zhang, Liu & Wang (2022). Micromachines 13, 1810. Machine-Learning-Enabled Design and Manipulation of a Microfluidic Concentration Gradient Generator. Informs six adjustable inlet flows/concentrations, the 2 × 8 mm chamber and D = 425 µm²/s. The preset converts Table 1 velocities using its 400 µm inlet width and an explicitly assumed 100 µm depth. This app uses an independent transport solver, not the paper's ANN.
    3. Garstecki et al. (2006). Lab on a Chip 6, 437–446. T-junction low-capillary-number squeezing scaling.
    4. Takken & Wille (2022). Sensors 22, 5392. Hydraulic resistance / pressure-flow circuit abstraction and its limits.

    No trained weights accompanied the PDFs. No paper-reported neural-network accuracy or experimental performance is claimed for this app. Confirm predictions for the intended fluids, wetting, fabrication and operating conditions.
